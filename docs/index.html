How to read these figures

Figures retrieved between 09:54:26 and 09:54:51 HKT (20:54 ET on 25 November).

Price type, session hours, time zones, and currencies

Hong Kong prices are last traded prices during the Thursday morning session and are delayed quotes, not official closes. US prices are official regular-session closes for Wednesday 25 November versus official closes on Tuesday 24 November. US cash equity markets were closed on Thursday 26 November for Thanksgiving. After-hours and overnight prints are not used. US Eastern times below are Eastern Standard Time (EST, UTC−5). All Hong Kong share prices are in Hong Kong dollars (HKD); all US share prices are in US dollars (USD).

Last price and official close.

For Hong Kong names, the last price is the most recent traded price in Thursday morning’s session — a snapshot, not the day’s official close. For US names, the figure shown is the official regular-session close: the exchange’s official end-of-day price on Wednesday 25 November. The prior official close is the official end-of-day price from the previous regular session (Tuesday 24 November for US names; Wednesday 25 November for Hong Kong names).

Open, high, and low.

The open is the first trade of that session. The high and low are the highest and lowest prices traded in that session (for Hong Kong, in the morning so far). The same idea applies to an index: its level is a single number that tracks the combined value of the stocks in the index, and the session high and low are the highest and lowest levels it reached.

Change.

The currency amount (HK dollars or US dollars, or index points) is how far the last price or official close sits from the previous official close. The percentage change is that difference expressed as a share of the previous official close. A rise of 4.31% means the price is 4.31% higher than that prior official close.

Market value (market cap).

This is a simple measure of a company’s size: the share price multiplied by the number of shares. It is stated in millions, billions, or trillions of the listing currency.

Trailing P/E.

This is the share price divided by the company’s earnings over the last 12 months. A higher figure means investors are paying more for each dollar of recent profit. If a company is not profitable on that basis, or if the quote did not provide a figure, no trailing P/E is shown.

52-week range.

These are the highest and lowest official prices over the past year. “Near the low,” “mid-range,” or “near the high” describes where the already-published last price or close sits between those two points.

Volume.

This is how many shares changed hands in the session. For Hong Kong names, it is only the morning session so far, so it is not compared with a typical full day’s trading. For US names, volume is also compared with a recent three-month daily average; a reading of 1.0 times means a typical day, and 1.84 times means trading was clearly heavier than usual.

50-day and 200-day averages.

These are the average official closing prices over the past 50 and 200 trading days, ending on Wednesday 25 November. They describe a medium-term and a longer-term typical level for the share. A last price or close above the average is higher than that recent typical level; one below it is lower. Thursday’s Hang Seng last prices were not included in these averages; where it matters, the write-up says where Thursday’s last sits relative to averages that stop at Wednesday’s official close.

14-day RSI.

This is a common 0–100 gauge of how strongly a share has risen or fallen over the past two weeks of official closes, also ending on Wednesday 25 November. Readings near 70 or higher mean recent gains have been strong; readings near 30 or lower mean recent declines have been strong. It describes recent movement; it is not a forecast.

Other company and market figures.

Deliveries are vehicles handed over to customers. Revenue is sales. Gross margin is the share of sales left after the direct cost of the goods sold. A net loss means costs exceeded income in that period. Brent and New York (WTI) crude are widely quoted international oil-futures prices, in US dollars per barrel.
