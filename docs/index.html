How to read these figures

Price type, session hours, time zones, and currencies

Hong Kong prices are official regular-session closes for Friday 27 November versus official closes on Thursday 26 November. US prices are official regular-session closes for Wednesday 25 November versus official closes on Tuesday 24 November — the latest completed US regular session, not Friday 27 November data. Thursday 26 November was a full US market close for Thanksgiving. Friday 27 November’s early US close (13:00 ET / 02:00 HKT on Saturday 28 November) had not yet begun when these US figures were confirmed. After-hours and overnight prints are not used. US Eastern times below are Eastern Standard Time (EST, UTC−5). All Hong Kong share prices are in Hong Kong dollars (HKD); all US share prices are in US dollars (USD)

Official close.

The figure shown for each share or index is the official regular-session close: the exchange’s official end-of-day price. For Hong Kong names, that is Friday 27 November versus the official close on Thursday 26 November. For US names, that is Wednesday 25 November versus the official close on Tuesday 24 November, the latest completed US regular session. Earlier last prices, pre-market prints, and after-hours prints are not used.

Open, high, and low.

The open is the first trade of that session. The high and low are the highest and lowest prices traded in that session. The same idea applies to an index: its level is a single number that tracks the combined value of the stocks in the index, and the session high and low are the highest and lowest levels it reached.

Change.

The currency amount (HK dollars or US dollars, or index points) is how far the official close sits from the previous official close. The percentage change is that difference expressed as a share of the previous official close. A rise of 8.21% means the official close is 8.21% higher than that prior official close.

Market value (market cap).

This is a simple measure of a company’s size: the share price multiplied by the number of shares. It is stated in millions, billions, or trillions of the listing currency.

Trailing P/E.

This is the share price divided by the company’s earnings over the last 12 months. A higher figure means investors are paying more for each dollar of recent profit. If a company is not profitable on that basis, or if the quote did not provide a reliable figure, no trailing P/E is shown.

52-week range.

These are the highest and lowest official prices over the past year. “Near the low,” “mid-range,” or “near the high” describes where the official close sits between those two points.

Volume.

This is how many shares changed hands in the official session. Volume is also compared with a recent three-month daily average; a reading of 1.0 times means a typical day, and 3.22 times means trading was clearly heavier than usual.

50-day and 200-day averages.

These are the average official closing prices over the past 50 and 200 trading days. For Hong Kong names, those averages end on Friday 27 November. For US names, they end on Wednesday 25 November. They describe a medium-term and a longer-term typical level for the share. A close above the average is higher than that recent typical level; one below it is lower.

14-day RSI.

This is a common 0–100 gauge of how strongly a share has risen or fallen over the past two weeks of official closes, ending on Friday 27 November for Hong Kong names and on Wednesday 25 November for US names. Readings near 70 or higher mean recent gains have been strong; readings near 30 or lower mean recent declines have been strong. It describes recent movement; it is not a forecast.

Other company and market figures.

Revenue is sales. A net loss means costs exceeded income in that period. Brent and New York (WTI) crude are widely quoted international oil-futures prices, in US dollars per barrel.

Percentage change among the top 10.

These charts show the already-published percentage change for each name in that index’s top 10, in the same rank order as the briefing.

These charts show each company’s size on that index’s top 10 list in US dollars. The length of each bar uses a scale that can show both the largest and the smallest company on that list. Read the labeled amounts for the actual size. A bar that looks twice as long is not necessarily twice as large.

Trailing P/E, where a figure is shown.

Trailing P/E is the share price divided by the company’s earnings over the last 12 months. On the Dow Jones Industrial Average chart, all ten names have a trailing P/E and all ten appear. The Nasdaq Composite has no trailing P/E chart because no trailing P/E is shown for any of its ten names.
